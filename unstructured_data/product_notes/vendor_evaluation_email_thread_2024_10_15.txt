Build vs buy is the eternal question. Mayo can afford to build because they have scale and talent density. For Cleveland Clinic, the math worked out in favor of Health Catalyst because:

1. DOS implementation was $800K but saved us from a $1.2M data warehouse build
2. Population Builder replaced 3 FTEs worth of manual cohort extraction
3. Healthcare.AI models would have taken 18+ months to build internally

That said, we're still dependent on Priya's team at Health Catalyst for model tuning. We don't have internal ML engineering depth. If they raised prices significantly or got acquired, we'd be in a tough spot.

Site visit - happy to host. November 12-14 works for our team. Amanda Rodriguez (our Chief Analytics Officer) can walk you through the Healthcare.AI clinical workflows. She's been the champion internally and has good perspective on physician adoption challenges.

Re: Tableau vs Cogito vs Health Catalyst - they serve different purposes:
- Cogito: Operational reporting, Epic-native, limited to Epic data
- Tableau: Visualization layer, works with any data source, requires underlying data architecture
- Health Catalyst DOS: Data platform, does ETL/integration/analytics, can feed Tableau or replace Cogito for some use cases

We actually have all three. Cogito for real-time operational dashboards, DOS + Population Builder for population health and research, Tableau for executive and board reporting (Finance team loves it). HealtheIntent (Cerner's population health tool) was in the mix but we sunset it when we went with Health Catalyst. The Cerner tools don't get much investment anymore since the Oracle acquisition.

Emily - on Waystar, I'd be careful. We looked at them as a backup clearinghouse and found their claim editing rules were less sophisticated than Change Healthcare. Your denial rate increase isn't surprising. The Intelligent Healthcare Network has much better payer rule libraries because of their market share. It's a classic chicken-and-egg problem for new entrants.

- Michael
