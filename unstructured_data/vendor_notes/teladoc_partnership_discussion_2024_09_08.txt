INTERNAL NOTES - CONFIDENTIAL
==============================
Re: Teladoc Health Partnership Discussion
Date: September 8, 2024
Author: Emily Davis (VP of Health IT, Johns Hopkins)
Distribution: Executive Leadership Only

BACKGROUND
----------
Met with Teladoc leadership to discuss expanding our virtual care capabilities beyond basic urgent care. Current contract is $450K/year for general medical visits only. They're proposing a comprehensive virtual care platform expansion.

STRATEGIC OPPORTUNITY
--------------------
Teladoc is offering Johns Hopkins a "Flagship Academic Partnership" designation - we'd be their showcase academic medical center for integrated virtual care. This comes with:

1. Preferential pricing (30% below standard rates)
2. Co-development of specialized virtual care programs
3. Joint research opportunities and publications
4. Dedicated clinical and technical support team

They're only offering this to 3-4 health systems nationally. Cleveland Clinic and UCSF are already in discussions.

PROPOSED EXPANSION
------------------

A. Mental Health Services ($380K/year)
   - 24/7 therapy and psychiatry access for our patient population
   - Average wait time: <48 hours for therapy, <72 hours for psychiatry
   - Integration with Epic for medication orders and documentation
   - Key differentiator: They have child/adolescent specialists (gap in our current network)

B. Chronic Care Management ($520K/year)
   - Remote monitoring for diabetes, hypertension, CHF, COPD
   - Includes patient devices (BP monitors, glucometers, pulse ox)
   - Dedicated care team (RN + dietitian + pharmacist) for high-risk patients
   - Claims data suggests 15-20% reduction in ED visits for enrolled patients

C. Specialty Virtual Consults ($290K/year)
   - Second opinions for complex cases
   - Pre-op consultations to reduce in-person visit burden
   - Post-discharge follow-ups
   - Could significantly reduce no-show rates and improve access

Total Investment: $1.19M/year (in addition to current $450K = $1.64M total)

BUSINESS CASE
-------------
Sarah Kim from finance ran preliminary numbers:

Revenue Opportunity:
- Reimbursement for virtual visits: Est. $850K-$1.1M annually
- Improved patient capture (vs. losing to competitors): $2-3M
- Reduced no-shows and late cancellations: $400K

Cost Avoidance:
- ED diversion for chronic care patients: $750K-$1.2M
- Reduced need to expand physical clinic space: $500K capital avoidance
- Staff overtime reduction (after-hours coverage): $180K

ROI appears strongly positive, but highly dependent on patient adoption rates and payer mix.

CONCERNS & RISKS
----------------

1. PHYSICIAN RESISTANCE
   - Some faculty are skeptical of virtual care quality
   - Concern about losing patient relationships to Teladoc clinicians
   - Fear of reduced RVU generation

   Mitigation: Position as access expansion, not replacement. Pilot with supportive departments first.

2. TECHNICAL INTEGRATION COMPLEXITY
   - Teladoc API integration with Epic is "bi-directional" but several customers report issues
   - Clinical documentation from Teladoc visits needs to flow seamlessly into Epic
   - Patient identity matching has been problematic at other sites

   Mitigation: Require proof-of-concept integration in test environment before contract signature.

3. BRAND RISK
   - Teladoc clinicians represent Johns Hopkins brand during patient interactions
   - We don't control their hiring, training, or quality oversight
   - Any patient safety events reflect on us even if Teladoc is liable

   Mitigation: Negotiate quarterly quality reviews, patient satisfaction minimums, and off-ramp clause.

4. FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY
   - Reimbursement for virtual visits is evolving - current rates may not persist
   - CMS signaled potential payment cuts for virtual care in 2026
   - Business case assumes 65% patient adoption - what if it's lower?

   Mitigation: Structure contract with volume-based pricing tiers.

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
---------------------
Our competitors are moving fast on this:
- UMMS just expanded their Amwell contract ($2M investment)
- Penn Medicine launched their own virtual care platform (built in-house, $5M)
- Duke partnered with Teladoc last year (hearing positive feedback)

If we don't move, we risk losing tech-savvy patients to competitors with better virtual access.

ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS
-------------------

Option A: Build In-House
- Estimated cost: $3-4M initial investment + $800K annual operating costs
- Timeline: 18-24 months to launch
- Pros: Full control, keep all revenue, data ownership
- Cons: Significant upfront capital, implementation risk, need to build clinical coverage model

Option B: Partner with Amwell Instead
- Similar pricing to Teladoc
- Better Epic integration according to some references
- Smaller provider network, especially for mental health
- No "flagship partnership" opportunity

Option C: Narrow Partnership (Chronic Care Only)
- Start with just chronic care management ($520K)
- Prove ROI before expanding to other services
- Reduces risk but also limits opportunity

RECOMMENDATION
--------------
I recommend moving forward with the Teladoc Flagship Partnership, but with phased implementation:

Phase 1 (Q1 2025): Mental Health + Chronic Care Management
- These have clearest ROI and fill urgent gaps
- Cost: $900K + existing $450K = $1.35M
- 6-month evaluation period with defined success metrics

Phase 2 (Q3 2025): Specialty Virtual Consults (if Phase 1 succeeds)
- Add $290K
- Total investment: $1.64M

Key Contract Terms to Negotiate:
1. 90-day termination clause (not the standard 180 days)
2. Patient satisfaction must maintain >85% rating
3. Epic integration must meet defined technical requirements in 60-day POC
4. Quarterly business reviews with C-suite access
5. Cap on annual price increases at 3%
6. Co-marketing rights for recruitment and publications

NEXT STEPS
----------
1. Present to executive leadership team (Sept 15 meeting)
2. Get clinical leadership input (dept chairs meeting Sept 20)
3. Negotiate term sheet if approved (Oct)
4. Technical POC for Epic integration (Nov-Dec)
5. Contract signature target: January 2025
6. Go-live target: March 2025

I'll need executive sponsorship from Dr. Morrison (CMO) to drive physician adoption. This is a significant strategic investment but I believe it positions us competitively for the next 3-5 years of virtual care growth.
