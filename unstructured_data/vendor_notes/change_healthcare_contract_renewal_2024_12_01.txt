ALTERNATIVE VENDOR ANALYSIS
---------------------------

We conducted RFI with three competitors in October:

Option A: Waystar (formerly ZirMed)
- Quoted price: $1.65M/year (8% below current, 23% below Change proposal)
- Technology: Modern, cloud-native platform
- Integration: Requires 6-9 month implementation (RISK)
- References: Strong (talked to Stanford, Duke)
- Concerns: Smaller company, less mature denial management features

Option B: R1 RCM
- Quoted price: $2.4M/year (33% above current!)
- Model: Comprehensive RCM service (not just tech, includes staff augmentation)
- Not apples-to-apples comparison - different service model
- Would require RCM staff reductions to justify cost
- Not pursuing

Option C: Build In-House Solution
- Epic has native RCM tools we're not fully utilizing
- IT estimates $1.2M to build, $400K/year to maintain
- Total 3-year cost: $2.4M ($800K/year equivalent)
- Timeline: 18-24 months (RISK)
- Pros: Full control, data ownership, Epic-native
- Cons: Implementation risk, opportunity cost, may lack advanced features

Switching Cost:
- Waystar implementation: $250K + 6-9 months disruption
- In-house build: $1.2M + 18-24 months + organizational change management
- Staying with Change Healthcare: $0 switching cost (maintain continuity)

CHANGE HEALTHCARE CONCERNS
--------------------------

1. CYBERSECURITY BREACH (February 2024)
   - Major ransomware attack disrupted healthcare industry
   - Mayo Clinic impacted: 3-week delay in claims processing
   - Cost to us: ~$850K in delayed cash flow + manual workarounds
   - Change Healthcare response: Adequate but slow
   - Concerns about future risk

2. UNITEDHEALTH OWNERSHIP
   - Change Healthcare owned by UnitedHealth Group (our competitor/payer)
   - Potential conflict of interest
   - Claims data visibility concerns
   - Regulatory scrutiny (DOJ investigating UHG vertical integration)

3. SERVICE DEGRADATION POST-BREACH
   - Support response times increased from 2 hours → 6 hours avg
   - System uptime dropped from 99.7% → 99.1%
   - Some features still not fully restored
   - Staff confidence in platform has declined

4. PRICE INCREASE TIMING
   - 19% increase feels opportunistic given our lack of easy alternatives
   - We're locked in by integration complexity
   - Market power imbalance

NEGOTIATION STRATEGY
--------------------

Our Position:
- Valued partner, strong performance, want to continue relationship
- 19% increase is not acceptable given breach impact and market benchmarks
- Willing to commit to 3-year term for pricing stability
- Need service level improvements and guarantees

Target Outcome:
Year 1: $1.95M (+8.3% vs. current) - "market rate adjustment" acceptable
Year 2: $2.03M (+4% escalation)
Year 3: $2.11M (+4% escalation)
Total 3-Year: $6.09M (save $700K vs. their proposal)

Required Contractual Improvements:
1. Uptime SLA: 99.5% with financial penalties for breach
2. Support response time: <2 hours for critical issues (contractual requirement)
3. Business continuity: Demonstrated disaster recovery capability quarterly
4. Cybersecurity: Annual third-party security audit with results shared
5. Data ownership: Clear language that our data cannot be used for UHG competitive advantage
6. Termination: 90-day out clause if acquired or major service degradation

Enhanced Features Must Include:
- AI denial prediction (no extra cost)
- Prior auth automation enhancements (no extra cost)
- Price transparency module (mandatory for CMS compliance)
- Dedicated account team with escalation path

Walk-Away Position:
If Change Healthcare won't negotiate below $2.0M Year 1, we pursue Waystar alternative
- Risk: Implementation disruption
- Mitigation: 9-month implementation runway before current contract expires

RECOMMENDATION
--------------

Recommend: Negotiate with Change Healthcare, with Waystar as backup

Rationale:
1. Switching costs and risks are high
2. Change Healthcare performance (pre-breach) was strong
3. Integration complexity with Epic makes switching painful
4. But 19% increase is not justified - push back hard
5. Use Waystar quote as leverage
